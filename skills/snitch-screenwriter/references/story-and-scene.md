# Story and scene development

Load this Reference for a substantial treatment, scene outline, or structural diagnosis.

For a sequence, record its starting state, character attempt, resistance, result, and new
state. Across sequences, name the result that motivates or permits the next move. Simple
chronology may be intentional for observation, travel, or montage; ask what changes for
the viewer before labeling it dead weight. A connector word alone does not prove a link.

When a turn feels arbitrary, look for a missing reason to choose it, means to perform it,
relation of obstacle to aim, or consequence carried forward. Repair the missing piece
with the smallest change that preserves protected material. Add exposition only when a
later action or revelation needs that fact.

Track a character's tactic and understanding separately. A character may keep the same
desire while changing tactics, learn without improving, or refuse to change and pay a
cost. Show change in a choice under pressure, not a summary claiming an arc. For an
ensemble, give each major thread distinct pressure and a point where it affects the
shared outcome. Do not manufacture redemption in a supplied tragedy or static character.

When assessing an observational scene, trace what its image or choice enables later.
A repeated image may give a later line meaning even if it conveys no new plot fact.
Record the source scene and callback scene together; an isolated Pass on the first
scene does not demonstrate that the longer link was checked.

For each scene, ask what the viewer knows before and after; what someone tries; which
fact, reversal, or commitment changes the exit state; and why the next scene can now
occur. A silent scene can turn through behavior. Atmosphere earns time when it establishes
a rule, dread, intimacy, or expectation later used. If several scenes repeat the same
state, merge, cut, or change their function instead of merely changing location.

The resolution answers the central question on its own terms. Identify the earlier
choice, rule, limitation, object, or relationship that enables it. Establish a new
ability before it solves the conflict. Preserve deliberate ambiguity; distinguish an
unanswered thematic question from a missing causal step.
