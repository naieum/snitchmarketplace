# Integrating camera and light into direction

Read while directing a full scene or film. This is the director's decision check; focused
optics, lighting, and camera-feasibility work belongs to snitch-cinematography.

For each essential shot, answer: what story information does the audience need now, whose
experience anchors it, what must stay hidden, and which image carries the change? Then
state subject action, viewpoint relative to the set, shot size, camera stillness or move,
focus intent if relevant, world light source, and exit state. One held shot can cover a
whole turn. Additional moves and cuts must earn their complexity through the scene.

Separate a camera's world position from screen direction, and a camera move from a zoom,
focus change, or actor movement. A lens number without a camera format or tested space
cannot lock the intended view. For movement, state start, trigger, path, and end. For light,
keep window/practical-source direction fixed in world space across angles unless a cue
changes it. Mood does not excuse hiding required props, faces, or gestures.

Check the action axis and eyelines. A visible crossing can reorient the audience; an
unexplained crossing can make two people swap screen positions. Carry prop and door states
through reverse angles. Record whether a proposed setup needs location access, new artwork,
set extension, a rig, or a capability test. Where user limits conflict with a choice, keep
the story information and propose a concrete alternative.

The integrated plan gives the crew or specialist a reasoned brief, not unverified exposure,
focal length, power, render-control, or rigging values. A camera or animatic test must
confirm readability, focus, timing, and visual match.
