# Integrating world design, blocking, and performance

Read while directing a full scene or film. This is the scene-intent and playable-geography
check; a reusable world or asset bible belongs to snitch-productiondesign.

For each scene, record source beat, audience information, character objective and tactic,
central turn, and ending state. Place key entrances, exits, obstacles, windows/practicals,
objects, actor start/end marks, and the sightlines that govern reveals. Use a small labeled
layout when directions are ambiguous. A location proposal must support the supplied action;
mark unmeasured dimensions as assumptions.

Direct observable behavior: what a character tries, what cue changes the attempt, and what
the viewer can read. A reaction may be the scene's decisive action while another character
speaks. A handoff needs before, transfer, and after; naming the recipient's later possession
is insufficient. Define whose information stays hidden until which action or shot.

Give recurring characters, locations, costumes, and hero props stable IDs. Carry their
invariant appearance separately from temporary state such as a wet sleeve, open door, or
held key. Assign actual references only where supplied or inspected. When appearance
references conflict, name which trait each controls rather than silently blending them.

For live action, check actor/camera access and resets; for 2D, silhouettes, layer order,
and new views; for 3D, scale and rig range; for stop motion, access and repeatability; for
generated or mixed media, explicit shot entry/exit states. These are handoff questions,
not claims that sets, artwork, or clips have been built. If a blocking or design change
would alter a protected story fact, present it as a proposal.
