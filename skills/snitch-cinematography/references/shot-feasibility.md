# Coverage and shot feasibility

Read when a request needs a shootable or animatable sequence rather than an isolated image.
A shot that looks compelling on paper still needs the action, transitions, and space to work.

## Cover the beat

For each story beat, locate the shot that shows the action, the changed state, and any
reaction the viewer needs. One held shot can cover all three. Mark essential shots and
optional coverage separately. A master can establish geography and provide a fallback,
but is not mandatory when another shot performs those jobs. An insert must carry specific
information. A continuous take needs a planned attention path and playable blocking.

For each adjacent selected shot, test entry and exit states: subject position and facing,
look direction, prop hand/position, action phase, doors and lights, world light direction,
and screen direction. A cut on action matches compatible phases of one action rather than
repeating it whole. A visible move across the action axis can reorient the audience; an
unexplained cut across it may invert relationships. Intentional disorientation is an
available choice when the sequence makes its purpose clear.

## Feasibility questions

| Medium | What the shot may require |
|---|---|
| Live action | Camera path and operator/rig space, lens focus distance, performer safety/marks, fixture access, reflections, power, reset time, weather or daylight continuity |
| 2D animation | Unseen background area revealed by a pan, extra layers for parallax, clean silhouettes at target scale, artwork for reverse angles |
| 3D animation | Set geometry beyond current frame, rig range, camera collision, contact and light consistency, simulation time |
| Stop motion | Reach to puppets between frames, stable fixtures, repeatable marks, removable supports |
| Generated or mixed media | Separate shot phases, stable reference descriptions, start/end states, actual tool capability and manual match tests |

Name a missing capability as a test or open item. No equipment list or generator support is
known simply because a shot uses familiar language. When the user has a hard limit, offer a
specific shot or staging alternative preserving the key information. Do not quietly remove
a protected reveal or change the story.

Selected timeline duration is the sum of nonoverlapping picture intervals after accounting
for transitions once. Alternative angles and unused handles add source material, not cut
length. A dialogue line heard across the next picture belongs to one timeline span. If
picture and audio labels cannot all be true, show the conflict and conditional resolutions.
Setup time affects production planning, not screen duration. Estimates become verified only
after a timed performance, animatic, or inspected edit.

## Handoff to the director

Give one recommended sequence and list unresolved options. For each shot, identify purpose,
start/end frame and state, camera/light plan, estimated time, adjacent transition, and
feasibility question. The director resolves any conflict with performance, world design,
edit, or sound. Do not claim the camera department has approved a change to dialogue,
blocking, costume, set, or ending just because it makes the shot easier.
