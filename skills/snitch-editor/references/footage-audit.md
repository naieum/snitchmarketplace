# Auditing an actual sequence

Read when source clips, a rough cut, stills, audio, or an edit decision list are supplied.
First inventory what can be opened: clip IDs, durations, timebases if available, audio
tracks, and any cut/export version. Inspect the requested spans and nearby handles needed
to test a cut. If only thumbnails or descriptions are accessible, limit conclusions to
what they actually show or state.

Compare the inspected assembly with the intended beat chain. For each important transition,
observe where picture and sound enter and leave, whether the action or gaze matches, and
whether the viewer has time to register the decisive information. Quote the observable
frame/action or audible cue with source ID and timestamp/range. Do not infer that an absent
beat is unavailable across all footage unless the provided source inventory has been
searched. Distinguish **missing from this cut**, **not found in inspected source**, and
**not captured according to the supplied inventory**.

Separate four kinds of note:

- **Story:** cause, reveal, decision, or response is unreadable.
- **Continuity:** position, action phase, prop state, screen direction, or sound perspective
  conflicts across a cut without serving a stated intention.
- **Rhythm:** a hold ends before information lands, or continues after the next useful
  change; support this with the inspected span, not a universal length rule.
- **Execution:** source handles, clipped audio, technical damage, or format constraints
  prevent the proposed selection. State what was actually tested.

A timeline screenshot can prove the existence and order of visible clips, not how their
performance reads. A silent export cannot support a sound judgment. A single still cannot
prove movement or duration. If the available media fails to support a verdict, make a
**Skip** with the missing span or format and continue with supported findings.
