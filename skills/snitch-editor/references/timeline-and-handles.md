# Selected timeline and source handles

Read when proposing an assembly or revising cut points. The selected timeline is the
sequence the audience will see; the source list is what production captured or generated.
Keep those lists separate even if every planned shot has only one take.

## Time ledger

For each selected segment, record source ID, source in and out when known, resulting screen
duration, and picture start in the assembly. A source clip may contain pre-roll or post-roll
handles that enable a trim, transition, or audio lead; a listed handle is usable only when
the actual source includes it. Do not treat requested handles as captured handles. If only
script or board timings exist, mark them estimates and compare their sum with the target.
Account for holds and transitions that change picture duration; do not add two overlapping
images or audio tails as two consecutive screen spans.

Timecode depends on the actual clip timebase and any drop-frame convention. Without that
metadata, use elapsed seconds or relative source positions, not invented `HH:MM:SS:FF`
precision. If clips have mixed frame rates, keep their source positions in their own
timebases until an editing system conforms them. An audio lead can begin before its image;
an audio tail can cross into the next image without lengthening the picture assembly.

## Cut points

At each boundary, ask what the outgoing frame completes, what the incoming frame begins,
and what the audience can infer between them. Check action phase, screen position and
travel, gaze, prop holder, light/time state, and whether a sound bridge helps the intended
connection. A jump, mismatch, or discontinuity may be expressive; name the intention and
test whether the audience can still follow the event.

If a cut needs material before or after a selected take, verify those frames or mark the
need as unverified. An alternate take may solve one match while losing an essential
reaction; compare the trade-off. A pickup brief identifies exactly what action or
information is absent, its entry and exit states, compatible eyeline/screen direction,
and minimum sound or prop continuity. It is a request to capture material, not evidence
that the material exists.
